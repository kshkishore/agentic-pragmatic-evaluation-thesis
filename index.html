Agentic Evaluation of Pragmatic Competence in Multimodal Large Language Models

A Prompt Engineering Approach · Katikela Sreeharsha Kishore · University of Hyderabad, 2026

This page runs the thesis analysis in your browser. There is nothing to install: Python, the data and the analysis scripts are loaded into the page, and every number is computed on your own computer.

Reproduce

Re-run the repository's own scripts on the logs of all 4,720 model calls. The page recomputes every statistic, checks each value against the published results, rebuilds the Chapter 4 tables and redraws the figures.

The study in brief

  • 160 items in ten categories of pragmatic phenomena: idioms, indirect speech acts, implicature, irony, deixis, register, politeness, empathy, intercultural pragmatics and charts.
  • Each item has a user prompt, a six-field CO-STAR specification, a reference and three responses:
    • A, appropriate;
    • B, a pragmalinguistic failure that reuses the reference's wording;
    • C, a sociopragmatic failure.
    Together they give 320 contrasts with known orderings.
  • Two open-weight judges, Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct and Phi-4-mini-instruct, were compared with 17 automatic metrics. A further experiment applied the judges to the replies of two generators.
  • Everything ran on a two-core CPU with deterministic, grammar-constrained decoding.

What runs in the browser: the analysis of the logged model calls (Level 1 in docs/REPRODUCING.md). Re-running the language models themselves (about 9 GB of model files and 12 hours) or the neural metrics requires a local Python installation. The repository explains how.

Reproduce the thesis results

Press Run to execute the repository's analysis in this page. On first use the browser downloads about 40 MB (Python and its scientific packages, which are then cached) plus 5 MB of data. The analysis itself takes about 2–5 minutes, depending on your computer. Keep this tab open while it runs.
